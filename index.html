How this works. Every animal here sits on one big family tree. To find a common ancestor, both animals are traced back up the tree until the two paths land on the same branch. That branch is the most recent ancestor they share — the last animal that was a great-great-great-(and so on)-grandparent of both.

The dates are rough estimates from fossils and DNA, so treat them as "roughly this long ago" rather than exact numbers. The tree follows how scientists group animals today, which has a few surprises in it: birds count as dinosaurs, whales sit inside the hoofed animals, and termites are a kind of cockroach.
