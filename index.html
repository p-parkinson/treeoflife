How the common ancestor is found: every animal in the built-in dataset hangs off a shared classification tree (a modern, clade-based backbone). Both lineages are traced back to the root and the deepest node they share is their most recent common ancestor (MRCA).

Divergence ages are rough, rounded consensus estimates for the crown group and are shown only for major clades — treat them as orders of magnitude, not measurements. Classification follows widely used modern phylogeny (so, for example, birds sit inside the reptiles and whales inside the even-toed ungulates), which is why some groupings differ from older textbooks.
